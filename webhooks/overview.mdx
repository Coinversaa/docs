**Webhooks** push the same data the [WebSocket](/websocket/overview) streams, to a URL you own, so you do not have to hold a socket open. You create a **subscription** — a **type** (which feed), its `args` (which wallet — or up to 1,000 wallets — or which market), optional `filters` (which frames you want) and the `url` to deliver to — and every matching frame is POSTed to that URL, signed with the subscription's own **signing secret**.

What arrives is the **raw source frame** — the same `channel`, `seq` and `data` the WebSocket sends — plus the `subscriptionId` it matched, with `data` cut down to the events that passed your filters. There is no envelope, no summary and no derived fields: a client that already reads the WebSocket feeds reads a webhook body unchanged.

## Who it is for

- **Servers and bots** that would rather be called than poll or hold a connection — a Lambda, a Cloud Run service, a Zapier / Make / n8n catch hook.
- **Alerting**: a wallet's fills above a size, a liquidation on a watched account, a price crossing a level.
- **Agents** that manage subscriptions from chat through the [MCP tools](/webhooks/agents).

## The six types

| Type | What it delivers | `args` | Filters | Minimum plan |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `tradesByUser` | Every fill of one wallet, or of up to 1,000 wallets, TWAP fills included | `user` | `coin`, `side`, `px`, `sz`, `closedPnl` | Free data* |
| `twapSliceFillsByUser` | The fills a wallet's TWAP orders executed | `user` | `coin`, `side`, `px`, `sz`, `closedPnl` | Free data* |
| `twapStatusesByUser` | The node's raw TWAP status records for one wallet or up to 1,000 | `user` | `coin`, `status`, `sz`, `executedSz`, `executedNtl`, `minutes` | Pro |
| `liquidations` | Every liquidation on the exchange | none | `coin`, `side`, `px`, `sz`, `closedPnl` | Agent |
| `liquidationsByUser` | Liquidations of one account, or of up to 1,000 (the liquidated side) | `user` | `coin`, `side`, `px`, `sz`, `closedPnl` | Agent |
| `priceCross` | A market's mark or trade price crossing a threshold | `coin`, `threshold`, `source`, `direction` | none — the condition is in `args` | Free data* |

\* Free-tier data, but Free's subscription allowance is **zero**, so creating one takes Starter or above. Feed access and the subscription allowance are separate checks — see [Plan limits](/webhooks/limits).

On the four by-user types `user` is one address or an array of 1 to 1,000 addresses — one subscription, one allowance slot, for many wallets ([one wallet or many](/webhooks/events#one-wallet-or-many)).

Every type, its arguments, filters and a delivery example are on [Subscription types](/webhooks/events). The machine-readable version is `GET /v1/subscriptions/definitions`, which lists the types **your** plan may create with their `args`, `filters` and `minTier` — read it instead of hard-coding this table ([reference](/webhooks/api#definitions)).

## How it relates to the WebSocket feeds

Same data, pushed to a URL. A `tradesByUser` subscription delivers the frames a `tradesByUser` WebSocket subscription would stream, filtered; `liquidations` and `liquidationsByUser` deliver liquidation objects; `twapStatusesByUser` delivers the node's status records. `priceCross` is the one type with no WebSocket twin: it watches a market's mark or trade price and synthesises its own `channel: "priceCross"` frame when the price crosses your threshold.

Two differences from the socket. A webhook body carries **`subscriptionId`**, which a WebSocket data frame does not, so one receiver can tell its subscriptions apart. And `data` holds **only the events that matched your filters** — a frame with no match is not delivered at all.

Treat `seq` as a per-source position, as on the socket: trade prices use the block height, mark prices use the replica's Unix nanoseconds — never compare the two ([sequence numbers](/websocket/protocol#sequence-numbers)).

## Delivery model

Each delivery is one `POST` to the subscription's `url`, signed the **[Standard Webhooks](https://www.standardwebhooks.com)** way: `webhook-id`, `webhook-timestamp` and `webhook-signature` headers, `v1` HMAC-SHA256 over `id.timestamp.body`, verified by the off-the-shelf `standardwebhooks` library for your language ([Signatures and delivery](/webhooks/signatures)).

- Any **2xx** acknowledges. Anything else is retried on a bounded back-off, respecting your `Retry-After` up to 24 hours, for up to **nine attempts over about 76 hours**.
- Retries are kept **in memory**: a restart of the service drops them. Only `priceCross` deliveries sit in a durable outbox that survives restarts.
- Delivery is **at least once**, and order across deliveries and retries is not guaranteed. Deduplicate on `webhook-id`.
- A **410** from your URL disables the subscription until you re-enable it with `POST /v1/subscriptions/{id}/enable`.
- The URL must be public **HTTPS on port 443**; redirects and private or reserved addresses are refused when you create the subscription.

Nothing is stored after a delivery is acknowledged or gives up: there is no delivery history, no replay and no test send. If you need to see what your receiver got, log it on your side.

## Subscriptions are immutable

A subscription's `type`, `args`, `filters` and `url` cannot be edited. To change one, delete it and create it again — which also resets a `priceCross` subscription's crossing baseline. Each subscription has one signing secret, returned once at creation and readable later with `GET /v1/subscriptions/{id}/secret`.

Subscriptions belong to your **account**, not to the API key that created them: any of your keys can list, delete or re-enable them. Your plan caps how many you can have — see [Plan limits](/webhooks/limits).

## Where to manage it

- The **developer portal** at [developers.coinversa.ai](https://developers.coinversa.ai).
- The **REST API** at `https://webhook.coinversa.ai` with `Authorization: Bearer <your API key>` — see the [API reference](/webhooks/api).
- The **MCP server** — tools to read the definitions and to create, list and delete subscriptions from chat; see [Webhooks for agents](/webhooks/agents).
