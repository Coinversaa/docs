**Webhooks** turn Coinversa Pulse from something you poll into something that calls you. You write a **rule** — which events, which wallets or coins, which thresholds — and every match becomes an **event**: one JSON envelope with a one-sentence `summary` for an LLM or a human, a typed `data` block for code, an honest `freshness` stamp, and the MCP tools that answer the obvious next question.

Events describe public on-chain activity. They are **data, not advice**: a `summary` says what happened, never what to do about it, and every envelope carries the same disclaimer — `"Descriptive market data from public on-chain activity. Not investment advice."` Nothing here trades, signs or touches an exchange account.

## What fires

| Family | Event types | Plan |
|---|---|---|
| Watched-wallet positions | `wallet.position.opened`, `.closed`, `.flipped`, `.increased`, `.decreased`, `.reconciled` | Free (Telegram); inbox and webhook from Starter |
| Watched-wallet liquidations | `wallet.liquidated`, `wallet.liquidation_risk` | Free (Telegram); inbox and webhook from Starter |
| Watched-wallet TWAP fills | `wallet.twap.filled` | Starter |
| Watched-wallet TWAP status | `wallet.twap.status` | Agent |
| Busy watched wallets | `wallet.activity.digest` — one summary every 5 min in place of a noisy wallet's individual events | Starter, on rules with an inbox or webhook channel (and the rule's other channels); Telegram-only rules get every event |
| Price levels | `market.price.crossed` (`above`, `below` or `both`) | Starter |
| Exchange-wide flow | `market.liquidation.large`, `market.trade.large` | Agent |
| Cohort positioning | `cohort.bias_flipped` | Agent |
| Sent by us | `webhook.test`, `webhook.rate_limited` | Starter (not subscribable) |

Every type, its filters and a full sample payload are in the [event catalogue](/webhooks/events). The same catalogue is served, unauthenticated, at `GET https://backend.coinversa.ai/api/v1/webhooks/event-types`.

## Three ways to receive

| Channel | Rule channel | You need | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Webhook POST** | `{"kind":"webhook","endpointId":"whe_…"}` | A public HTTPS endpoint (Starter and above) | Servers, bots, Zapier / Make / n8n catch hooks |
| **Inbox pull** | `{"kind":"inbox"}` | Nothing — read with `GET /events` or the `pulse_events` MCP tool | Agents with no server, scheduled Claude routines, backfilling after downtime |
| **Telegram** | `{"kind":"telegram","chatId":"…"}` | The Coinversa alerts bot (its own `/activate` flow; not yet attachable from the API, portal or MCP) | Humans; the only channel on Free |

The **inbox records every matched event, whatever its channels** — a webhook rule's events are pullable too, so a receiver that was down can catch up with a cursor instead of waiting for retries. Use `{"kind":"inbox"}` alone when you want pull-only delivery. Inbox and webhook delivery count against your plan's monthly event budget; see [Plan limits](/webhooks/limits).

## Freshness, per source

Each envelope's `freshness.source` names where the event came from and `freshness.typicalLagMs` is the typical delay between the on-chain moment (`occurredAt`) and when we saw it (`observedAt`). They differ by source, and the event says which:

| `freshness.source` | Event types | `typicalLagMs` | What it means |
|---|---|---:|---|
| `fills_stream` | `wallet.position.opened` … `.decreased`, `wallet.twap.filled`, `market.trade.large` | 500 | Fills from the Coinversa fill stream, coalesced per block |
| `liquidations_stream` | `wallet.liquidated`, `market.liquidation.large` | 500 | Liquidation frames from the stream |
| `mids_feed` | `market.price.crossed` | 1,000 | Evaluated on every mid-price tick |
| `live_positions` | `wallet.position.reconciled` | 5,000 | A position reseed after a stream gap — the corrected state, not the missed fills |
| `info_api_poll` | `wallet.liquidation_risk` | 30,000 | A poll of Hyperliquid's Info API every 30 s — this event can be up to a poll interval late |
| `twap_status_stream` | `wallet.twap.status` | 500 | The node's TWAP status events from the stream |
| `cohort_poll` | `cohort.bias_flipped` | 60,000 | A poll of the live cohort bias every 60 s — up to a poll interval late |
| `digest` | `wallet.activity.digest` | 300,000 | A 5-minute roll-up of a noisy wallet's suppressed events |
| `synthetic` | `webhook.test`, `webhook.rate_limited` | 0 | Generated by us; no chain moment |

`typicalLagMs` is a typical value carried in the payload, not a guarantee. Compare `occurredAt` and `observedAt` on the event you received for its actual lag, and `GET /plan` reports measured [latency per event type](/webhooks/api#plan).

## Delivery model

Delivery works the way Stripe's does, signed the **[Standard Webhooks](https://www.standardwebhooks.com)** way. Each POST carries `webhook-id`, `webhook-timestamp` and `webhook-signature` (HMAC-SHA256 over the id, the timestamp and the raw body), so an off-the-shelf `standardwebhooks` library verifies it; any **2xx within 5 seconds** is success, a `3xx` is a failure that is never followed or retried, and anything else is retried on a fixed schedule — immediately, then after 30 s, 2 min, 10 min, 1 h and 6 h — before the delivery is marked dead and kept in the log for **replay**. Delivery is **at least once**, so deduplicate on `webhook-id` (also `id` in the body). A `410 Gone` disables the endpoint at once; 20 consecutive dead deliveries or 72 hours of failures disable it automatically, events keep landing in the inbox meanwhile, and a **recovery probe** re-enables it on its own once it answers again. The details are on [Signatures and delivery](/webhooks/signatures).

Storage is guarded so one noisy rule cannot bury the rest: events past your monthly budget or per-minute burst are dropped and counted in one hourly `webhook.rate_limited` event, a wallet producing more than 120 events an hour for a rule is folded into 5-minute digests, and `POST /rules?dryRun=1` estimates a rule's events per day before you save it. See [Plan limits](/webhooks/limits).

## Building for your own users

If you run alerts for your own customers, Coinversa is the plumbing, not the brand. Create **one rule per customer**, put your customer's name or reference in the rule's `label`, and route on `rule.id`: every envelope carries `rule: { "id", "label" }`, so one endpoint can fan events out to the right customer without parsing `data`. Key your routing on `rule.id` — the 24-character id is fixed for the rule's life, while a `label` can be edited — and show `rule.label` or your own wording to the customer. The `summary` is a neutral sentence about on-chain activity that you can pass through or rewrite; keep the descriptive, not-advice framing of `disclaimer` when you do.

## Where to manage it

The same operations exist in three places, against the same data:

- The **developer portal** — the Webhooks page at [developers.coinversa.ai](https://developers.coinversa.ai): endpoints, rules and an activity log with replay.
- The **REST API** at `https://backend.coinversa.ai/api/v1/webhooks` with your `X-API-Key` — see the [API reference](/webhooks/api).
- The **MCP server** — six tools to read the inbox, discover event types and manage rules from chat; see [Webhooks for agents](/webhooks/agents). Creating an endpoint is portal or REST only, so a signing secret never passes through a chat transcript.
