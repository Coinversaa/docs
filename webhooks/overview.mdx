**Webhooks** push the same data the [WebSocket](/websocket/overview) streams, to a URL you own, so you do not have to hold a socket open. You create a **subscription** — a **type** (which feed), its `args` (which wallet — or up to 1,000 wallets — or which market), optional `filters` (which frames you want) and the `url` to deliver to — and every matching frame is POSTed to that URL, signed with the subscription's own **signing secret**. A subscription with `"deliveryMode": "inbox"` and no URL stores its matches in your account's **[inbox](/webhooks/inbox)** instead, for you or an agent to pull.

What arrives is the **raw source frame** — the same `channel`, `seq` and `data` the WebSocket sends — plus the `subscriptionId` it matched, with `data` cut down to the events that passed your filters. There is no envelope, no summary and no derived fields: a client that already reads the WebSocket feeds reads a webhook body unchanged. `priceCross`, the four cohort bias types and the four liquidation risk types are the exception: they build their own events, in the same frame ([below](#how-it-relates-to-the-websocket-feeds)).

## Who it is for

- **Servers and bots** that would rather be called than poll or hold a connection — a Lambda, a Cloud Run service, a Zapier / Make / n8n catch hook.
- **Alerting**: a wallet's fills above a size, a liquidation on a watched account, a price crossing a level, a trader cohort changing side on a coin.
- **Agents** that subscribe from chat through the [MCP tools](/webhooks/agents) and read what the subscriptions matched from the inbox, with no server at all.

## The types

Six event types:

| Type | What it delivers | `args` | Filters | Minimum plan |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `tradesByUser` | Every fill of one wallet, or of up to 1,000 wallets, TWAP fills included | `user` | `coin`, `side`, `px`, `sz`, `closedPnl` | Free data* |
| `twapSliceFillsByUser` | The fills a wallet's TWAP orders executed | `user` | `coin`, `side`, `px`, `sz`, `closedPnl` | Free data* |
| `twapStatusesByUser` | The node's raw TWAP status records for one wallet or up to 1,000 | `user` | `coin`, `status`, `sz`, `executedSz`, `executedNtl`, `minutes` | Pro |
| `liquidations` | Every liquidation on the exchange | none | `coin`, `side`, `px`, `sz`, `closedPnl` | Agent |
| `liquidationsByUser` | Liquidations of one account, or of up to 1,000 (the liquidated side) | `user` | `coin`, `side`, `px`, `sz`, `closedPnl` | Agent |
| `priceCross` | A market's mark or trade price crossing a threshold | `coin`, `threshold`, `source`, `direction` | none — the condition is in `args` | Free data* |

\* Free-tier data, but Free's subscription allowance is **zero**, so creating one takes Starter or above. Feed access and the subscription allowance are separate checks — see [Plan limits](/webhooks/limits).

On the four by-user types `user` is one address or an array of 1 to 1,000 addresses — one subscription, one allowance slot, for many wallets ([one wallet or many](/webhooks/events#one-wallet-or-many)).

And four **cohort bias** types, which report how a [trader cohort](/concepts/cohorts) is positioned on one coin — its net bias, from `-1` (all short) to `1` (all long):

| Type | What it delivers | `args` | Filters | Minimum plan |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `cohortBias` | The cohort rows of each new snapshot, and the current state once when the subscription is created | `coin`, `cohort`, `tierType` | `netBias`, `longNotional`, `shortNotional`, `accountCount` | Starter |
| `cohortBiasFlip` | A cohort's net bias going from long to short, or from short to long | `coin`, `cohort`, `direction`, `tierType` | `netBias`, `longNotional`, `shortNotional`, `accountCount` | Starter |
| `cohortBiasOpposition` | Two cohorts entering opposite positioning | `coin`, `cohort`, `otherCohort`, `tierType` | none — the condition is in `args` | Starter |
| `cohortBiasMove` | A cohort's net bias moving at least a number of percentage points | `coin`, `cohort`, `points`, `tierType` | `netBias`, `longNotional`, `shortNotional`, `accountCount` | Starter |

Cohort positioning is a stored snapshot, normally taken every 15 minutes, so these four tell you about a change **at the next snapshot** — not when a trade happens. See [Cohort bias types](/webhooks/cohort-bias).

And four **liquidation risk** types, which report potential liquidation exposure — two for one wallet's positions, two for a whole market.

| Type | What it delivers | `args` | Filters | Minimum plan |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `liquidationRiskCross` | A position of one wallet whose distance to its liquidation price crosses a threshold | `user`, `liquidationDistancePct`, `direction` | none — the condition is in `args` | Starter |
| `liquidationRisk` | A position of one wallet whose distance to its liquidation price has moved at least a number of percentage points | `user`, `points` | `liquidationDistancePct` | Starter |
| `liquidationExposure` | A market where at least a set notional has liquidation levels within a price move from the mark | `coin`, `priceMovePct`, `minNotional`, `direction`, `resetNotional` | none — the condition is in `args` | Starter |
| `liquidationMap` | A market's full liquidation map, after the mark has moved by a percentage | `coin`, `priceChangePct`, `buckets`, `range` | none — the condition is in `args` | Starter |

These are positions that are still open — not executed liquidations, which are `liquidations` and `liquidationsByUser` above. The service checks about once a minute, using positions and liquidation levels as last stored. On the two wallet types `user` is exactly one wallet. See [Liquidation risk types](/webhooks/liquidation-types).

Every type, its arguments and filters are on [Subscription types](/webhooks/events), with a delivery example for each event type; the cohort bias delivery examples are on [Cohort bias types](/webhooks/cohort-bias) and the liquidation risk ones on [Liquidation risk types](/webhooks/liquidation-types). The machine-readable version is `GET /v1/subscriptions/definitions`, which lists the types **your** plan may create with their `args`, `filters` and `minTier` — read it instead of hard-coding this table ([reference](/webhooks/api#definitions)).

## How it relates to the WebSocket feeds

Same data, pushed to a URL. A `tradesByUser` subscription delivers the frames a `tradesByUser` WebSocket subscription would stream, filtered; `liquidations` and `liquidationsByUser` deliver liquidation objects; `twapStatusesByUser` delivers the node's status records. `priceCross` has no WebSocket twin: it watches a market's mark or trade price and synthesises its own `channel: "priceCross"` frame when the price crosses your threshold. The four cohort bias types do not come from the fill stream at all: they are built from stored cohort positioning snapshots, and their `data` holds [cohort rows](/webhooks/cohort-bias#the-cohort-row) in the same `channel`, `seq`, `subscriptionId`, `data` frame. The four liquidation risk types are built the same way, from stored positions and liquidation levels ([delivered fields](/webhooks/liquidation-types#delivered-fields)).

Two differences from the socket. A webhook body carries **`subscriptionId`**, which a WebSocket data frame does not, so one receiver can tell its subscriptions apart. And `data` holds **only the events that matched your filters** — a frame with no match is not delivered at all.

Treat `seq` as a per-source position, as on the socket: trade prices use the block height, mark prices use the replica's Unix nanoseconds — never compare the two ([sequence numbers](/websocket/protocol#sequence-numbers)). The cohort bias and liquidation risk types use a third kind: the Unix microseconds at which the snapshot or observation was read.

## Delivery model

Each delivery is one `POST` to the subscription's `url`, signed the **[Standard Webhooks](https://www.standardwebhooks.com)** way: `webhook-id`, `webhook-timestamp` and `webhook-signature` headers, `v1` HMAC-SHA256 over `id.timestamp.body`, verified by the off-the-shelf `standardwebhooks` library for your language ([Signatures and delivery](/webhooks/signatures)).

- Any **2xx** acknowledges. Anything else is retried on a bounded back-off, respecting your `Retry-After` up to 24 hours, for up to **nine attempts over about 76 hours**.
- Retries are kept **in memory**: a restart of the service drops them. Only `priceCross`, cohort bias and liquidation risk deliveries sit in a durable outbox that survives restarts.
- Delivery is **at least once**, and order across deliveries and retries is not guaranteed. Deduplicate on `webhook-id`.
- A **410** from your URL disables the subscription until you re-enable it with `POST /v1/subscriptions/{id}/enable`.
- The URL must be public **HTTPS on port 443**; redirects and private or reserved addresses are refused when you create the subscription.

Nothing is stored after an HTTP delivery is acknowledged or gives up: there is no delivery history, no replay and no test send. If you need to see what your receiver got, log it on your side.

## Or pull from the inbox

If you have no server to receive a POST, create the subscription with `"deliveryMode": "inbox"` and **no `url`**. The frames it matches are stored in your account's one **inbox** — the same envelope, plus an `inboxId` and the time it was received — and you read them with `POST /v1/inbox/drain`, which deletes nothing, then `POST /v1/inbox/ack` for the ones you have processed. An inbox subscription takes the same slot as an HTTP one and has no signing secret; pending messages are capped per plan (Starter 1,000, Agent 50,000, Pro and Enterprise 1,000,000, Free none), kept for 14 days under a 2 GiB cap, with the oldest evicted on overflow. It is the delivery mode the [MCP tools](/webhooks/agents) use by default. See [Inbox delivery](/webhooks/inbox).

## Subscriptions are immutable

A subscription's `type`, `args`, `filters`, `deliveryMode` and `url` cannot be edited. To change one, delete it and create it again — which also resets a `priceCross` subscription's crossing baseline and a cohort bias or liquidation risk subscription's state. Each HTTP subscription has one signing secret, returned once at creation and readable later with `GET /v1/subscriptions/{id}/secret`; an inbox subscription has none.

Subscriptions belong to your **account**, not to the API key that created them: any of your keys can list, delete or re-enable them. Your plan caps how many you can have — see [Plan limits](/webhooks/limits).

## Where to manage it

- The **developer portal** at [developers.coinversa.ai](https://developers.coinversa.ai).
- The **REST API** at `https://webhook.coinversa.ai` with `Authorization: Bearer <your API key>` — see the [API reference](/webhooks/api).
- The **MCP server** — tools to create, list and delete inbox subscriptions and to read the inbox from chat; see [Webhooks for agents](/webhooks/agents).
