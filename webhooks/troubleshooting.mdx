Start from what you see. There is no delivery log on our side — nothing is stored after a delivery — so the places to look are `GET /v1/subscriptions` ([reference](/webhooks/api#list-subscriptions)), the unauthenticated `GET /health` ([reference](/webhooks/api#health)) and your own receiver's logs.

## `400` when creating a subscription

The body failed validation and nothing was saved. The usual causes:

1. **Impossible range.** `{"gt": "10", "lt": "5"}` can never match. Equal endpoints are allowed only when both are inclusive: `{"gte": "10", "lte": "10"}` is fine, `{"gt": "10", "lte": "10"}` is not.
2. **Unknown filter or operator.** Only the fields the type declares in `GET /v1/subscriptions/definitions` and only `gt`, `gte`, `lt`, `lte` are accepted — no `eq`, no `$gt`, no nested paths. `priceCross` takes no filters at all.
3. **A number that is not a decimal string.** `1.5` must be `"1.5"`; exponent notation, `NaN`, infinity and `1/2` are rejected; the maximum length is 128 characters.
4. **Unknown type or argument.** Check the spelling against the definitions; `user` sent both at the top level and inside `args` is also a `400`.
5. **A missing required argument.** `user` on the four by-user types, `coin` and `threshold` on `priceCross` (a positive decimal string).
6. **A bad wallet array.** `args.user` may be one address or an array of 1 to 1,000; an empty array, an invalid address or more than 1,000 entries is refused, as is a creation body over 64 KiB.

## `403` when creating a subscription

The type is above your plan: `liquidations` and `liquidationsByUser` need **Agent**, `twapStatusesByUser` needs **Pro**. The definitions endpoint only lists what your plan may create, so a type you did not see there will be refused. See [Types per plan](/webhooks/limits#types-per-plan).

## `429` when creating or enabling

{/* The /enable route and "disabled subscriptions do not count" depend on fix/webhook-enable-health being deployed. */}

Your account is at its subscription allowance — tentatively 2 on Starter, 6 on Agent, 10 on Pro, 20 on Enterprise, and **0 on Free**. Delete a subscription you no longer need (`GET /v1/subscriptions` to find it), or move up a plan. A disabled subscription does not count toward the allowance, which is why `POST /v1/subscriptions/{id}/enable` can be refused with `429` too. See [Plan limits](/webhooks/limits).

## Deliveries stopped and the subscription shows `disabled: true`

{/* Depends on fix/webhook-enable-health being deployed: POST /v1/subscriptions/{id}/enable is 404 until then. */}

Your URL answered **`410 Gone`** to a delivery. That disables the subscription at once; frames matched while it is disabled are not kept. Fix whatever returned the `410`, then:

```bash
curl -X POST https://webhook.coinversa.ai/v1/subscriptions/$SUB_ID/enable \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $COINVERSA_API_KEY"
```

The call is idempotent. If it answers `429`, you are at your allowance with the disabled one re-counted — delete another subscription first. A framework that answers `410` for an unknown route, or a proxy that does during a deploy, is the usual cause; make the route answer `404` or `503` instead for anything but a deliberate stop.

## Nothing arrived, and the service restarted

Ordinary deliveries and their retries are kept **in memory**. When the webhook service restarts — a deploy, a crash — whatever was queued or waiting to retry is lost, and nothing is replayed afterwards. Your subscription is still saved and starts delivering again on the next matching frame. If your receiver was down at the same time, the frames it missed are gone from the webhook side: read the wallet's fills from the [REST API](/quickstart) or the [WebSocket](/websocket/overview) to fill the gap.

`priceCross` behaves differently: its deliveries sit in a durable outbox and survive a restart, though a crossing that happened entirely during an outage of the **source** cannot be reconstructed.

## Nothing arrives, and the service did not restart

1. **Is the subscription there and enabled?** `GET /v1/subscriptions`. A `disabled: true` means a `410` — see above.
2. **Do the filters match?** Exact text is case-sensitive (`BTC`, not `btc`); `side` is `A` for sell and `B` for buy; sizes are in the market's base unit and prices in its quote, so a `sz` bound of `"100000"` on BTC never matches. A missing or null field never satisfies a numeric filter, so a `closedPnl` bound only matches fills that carry one.
3. **Is it the right wallet?** `liquidationsByUser` selects the **liquidated** account, not the liquidator's fills; `twapSliceFillsByUser` only carries fills with a `twapId`.
4. **Is your receiver acknowledging?** Only a `2xx` counts. A `200` after a long handler may already have timed out on our side and been retried; answer first, process after.
5. **Is the receiver's queue share full?** A subscription holds at most 16 slots across the fresh and retry pools; a failing receiver saturates its own share and its newest frames are dropped. Fix the receiver and the subscription resumes on its own.
6. **Is the service healthy?** `GET /health` answers `200` while the service is up, with service counts in the body ([reference](/webhooks/api#health)).

## The URL was refused

The `url` must be **public HTTPS on port 443**, reached without a redirect. Refused at creation:

| Cause | Fix |
|---|---|
| A private, loopback, link-local or otherwise reserved address — `localhost`, `127.0.0.1`, `10.x`, `192.168.x`, a host that resolves to one | Expose a public host. There is no `http://` dev-tunnel option; use a tunnel that terminates TLS publicly |
| `http://` | Use `https://` |
| A port other than 443 — `https://example.com:8443/hook` | Put the receiver behind a proxy on 443 |
| A redirect — the URL answers `3xx` | Register the final URL |

## Signature mismatch

Check your verifier against the [known-answer test vector](/webhooks/signatures#known-answer-test-vector) first — it isolates your code from your framework. Then, in order of how often each is the cause:

1. **The body was re-serialised.** You verified `JSON.stringify(req.body)`, not the bytes received. Use `express.raw`, `await request.body()`, `request.get_data()` or `io.ReadAll(r.Body)` on the webhook route.
2. **Wrong signed content.** It is `webhook-id + "." + webhook-timestamp + "." + body` — the id first.
3. **Wrong secret.** Each subscription has its own; if one receiver serves several, look the secret up by the body's `subscriptionId`. `GET /v1/subscriptions/{id}/secret` returns the current one.
4. **A hand-rolled HMAC.** Verify with a `standardwebhooks` library for your language; it takes the `whsec_…` secret as you received it and handles the key.
5. **Hex instead of base64.** The signature is standard base64, after the `v1,` prefix.
6. **Clock skew.** The libraries reject a `webhook-timestamp` more than five minutes from your clock. Run NTP.

## Duplicate deliveries

Expected: delivery is **at least once**, and order is not guaranteed across workers and retries. A retry follows any failure we observed, including a timeout after your handler already succeeded. Deduplicate on `webhook-id`, which is stable across the retries of one delivery, and order by `seq` within one source if order matters. If two different ids carry the same fill, check whether two of your subscriptions match it — each delivers on its own.
